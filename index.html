    Course information

    What is BUS 100?

    This course introduces first-year students to contemporary business and helps them build connections within the Scott College of Business.

    Catalog description

    Students survey business disciplines and contemporary issues, begin career development, learn team-building skills, and explore career-enriching experiences such as internships and study abroad. The course is not open to juniors or seniors in the College of Business.

    Business disciplines

    Accounting, finance, insurance and risk management, marketing, supply chain management, management, and human resource management.

    Required: Better Business, 7th ed. by Michael R. Solomon, Mary Anne Poatsy, and Kendall Martin.
    e-Textbook ISBN 978-0-13-543522-9 · Print ISBN 978-0-13-543504-5

    Recommended: selected articles, reports, and media available via Canvas.

    Course overview

    Why this course matters

    BUS 100 connects foundational business knowledge with academic success, professional development, and career decision-making.

    Build business knowledge

    Understand how organizations create value and address challenges in today's global and technology-driven environment.

    Explore your direction

    Identify the business major and career path that align with your interests, strengths, and goals.

    Develop professionally

    Assess skills, build teamwork and communication abilities, explore ethical business practices, and analyze emerging technologies such as artificial intelligence.

    Gain experience

    Experiential learning opportunities, including internships and study abroad, support academic and career readiness.

    Learning objectives

    What you should be able to do

    Upon successful completion of BUS 100, students will be able to:

    1. Apply academic success strategies and university resources to support personal responsibility, academic achievement, and degree completion.
    2. Develop an initial academic and career plan by identifying personal strengths, interests, business majors, and professional opportunities.
    3. Demonstrate professional behaviors and effective communication skills appropriate for academic, business, and workplace settings.
    4. Apply critical thinking, decision-making, and digital technologies, including artificial intelligence, to analyze business and professional situations.
    5. Explain the roles and interrelationships of the major business disciplines and their contributions to organizational success.
    6. Explain how economic, technological, ethical, social, cultural, and global factors influence business decisions and organizational performance.

    Assignments

    Reading, applied learning & quizzes

    Interactive Reading Assignments · 200 pts

    11 Pearson MyLab assignments, 20 points each. The lowest score is dropped, resulting in 200 points.

    Assignments · 170 pts

    Professional development and applied learning activities in Pearson MyLab, including simulations, case analyses, and media-based assignments.

    Chapter Quizzes · 200 pts

    11 quizzes, 20 points each, with the lowest score dropped. Each quiz has 25 true/false and multiple-choice questions in 50 minutes. Two attempts are allowed; the higher score counts.

    Professional Development · 130 pts

    Activities support academic, career, and personal growth; exploration of business careers; assessment of interests, strengths, and skills; and informed major and career decisions.

    Final Exam · 100 points
    Comprehensive case analyses, short-answer, and essay questions. No makeup test unless pre-arranged due to an unexpected circumstance.
    Grading policies

    Submission standards

    • Submit written assignments through the designated Canvas or Pearson MyLab link, following the specific instructions.
    • Proofread work and submit it in an appropriate format such as Microsoft Word, Excel, or PDF.
    • Use a consistent citation style whenever necessary.
    • Every written assignment must include the preparation date, course number/title, assignment title, and your name. Each missing or incomplete required item results in a 5% deduction; submissions missing all required items receive a 20% deduction.
    • Late assignments receive a 2% penalty per day and will not be accepted seven days after the due date unless an extraordinary circumstance is documented and approved.
    • Plagiarism in a written assignment results in a zero and may be reported to the university.
    • Assignments are expected to be graded within seven business days. If grading is not completed within seven business days and students are not informed of a delay with a definite completion date, a complete, timely, requirement-compliant submission receives full credit.
    Attendance

    Attendance bonus

    Students are expected to attend all class sessions and arrive on time. Attendance is monitored and can add up to 40 points to the final course points.

    No absences

    40 bonus points.

    Excused absence

    Beginning with the second excused absence, the bonus decreases by 4 points for each excused absence.

    Unexcused absence

    Beginning with the first unexcused absence, the bonus decreases by 8 points for each unexcused absence, starting with the second class.

    Important details

    Each late arrival or early departure deducts 1 point. Once all 40 bonus points are forfeited, no additional attendance deductions apply and the bonus cannot fall below 0. University- or instructor-canceled classes count as full attendance.

    To have an absence classified as excused, submit written documentation within one week. The instructor reviews and approves the documentation.

    Course policies

    Academic integrity, communication & technology

    Academic integrity

    Students are responsible for understanding plagiarism and Indiana State University academic integrity policies. Academic dishonesty in written assignments, quizzes, or the final exam may result in an F for the course. Plagiarism in a written assignment results in zero; plagiarism by a group member can result in zero for the group assignment when other members do not complete plagiarism.

    Communication

    Canvas Inbox is the official means of individualized communication. Class announcements may be made in class or through Canvas, and an announcement made in one location is not guaranteed to be repeated in the other. Weekday messages are normally answered within 24 hours; Friday, holiday-eve, weekend, and holiday messages may be answered the following Monday or business day.

    Students are responsible for having the necessary equipment, including audio/video capability, to participate fully. Devices may be used for class-related purposes only. The instructor may ask students to close devices and actively participate in class activities.

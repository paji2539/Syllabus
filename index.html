Catalog Description

An introduction to contemporary business and development of connections to the Scott College of Business for first-year students. Special attention will be given to a survey of business disciplines and contemporary issues in business. Students begin their career development process, learn team building skills, and explore career enriching experiences such as internships and study abroad. Not open to juniors or seniors in the College of Business.

Overview and Introduction

This course introduces first-year students to the dynamic world of business while helping them build meaningful connections within the Scott College of Business. Through an engaging survey of key business disciplines, including accounting, finance, insurance and risk management, marketing, supply chain management, management, and human resource management, you will gain an understanding of how organizations create value and address challenges in today's global and technology-driven environment. As you explore these disciplines, you will be better equipped to identify the business major and career path that align with your interests, strengths, and goals.

Accounting Finance Insurance & Risk Mgmt. Marketing Supply Chain Mgmt. Management Human Resource Mgmt.

BUS 100 also supports professional development. Students will assess their skills, build teamwork and communication abilities, explore ethical business practices, and analyze the impact of emerging technologies like artificial intelligence. Experiential learning opportunities, including internships and study abroad, are emphasized to foster academic and career readiness.

02 · Outcomes

Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of BUS 100, students will be able to:

  1. Apply academic success strategies and university resources to support personal responsibility, academic achievement, and degree completion.
  2. Develop an initial academic and career plan by identifying personal strengths, interests, business majors, and professional opportunities.
  3. Demonstrate professional behaviors and effective communication skills appropriate for academic, business, and workplace settings.
  4. Apply critical thinking, decision-making, and digital technologies, including artificial intelligence, to analyze business and professional situations.
  5. Explain the roles and interrelationships of the major business disciplines and their contributions to organizational success.
  6. Explain how economic, technological, ethical, social, cultural, and global factors influence business decisions and organizational performance.
  7. Evaluate the importance of ethics, social responsibility, diversity, and inclusion in business and professional environments.

05 · Coursework

Assignments & Exams

Chapter Learning Objective Notes

Chapter Learning Objective Notes are designed to help you actively engage with the assigned textbook readings by focusing on the learning goals (or purposes) of each chapter. Rather than simply summarizing the chapter, you should identify the key concepts, principles, and ideas that support each learning goal and explain how they contribute to achieving the chapter's intended outcomes. This approach encourages you to read with purpose, organize information around the most important takeaways, and develop a deeper understanding of the material.

By completing the Chapter Learning Objective Notes consistently, you will reinforce your understanding of the chapter's core concepts, improve long-term retention of the material, and strengthen your ability to apply course concepts to discussions, case analyses, assignments, real-world situations, and expand to other courses. Focusing on the learning objectives will also help you distinguish between essential concepts and supporting details, making your studying more effective and efficient.

Detailed instructions, expectations, and information regarding this assignment will be available on Canvas.

Case Analyses

Policy-specific and comprehensive property and liability risk management and insurance cases will be analyzed throughout the semester, with a strong emphasis on insurance claim analysis. These cases will require students to apply policy language, coverage forms, endorsements, exclusions, limits, and deductibles to realistic loss scenarios. Students will evaluate the facts of each claim, identify applicable coverages, determine whether coverage is triggered, and assess potential insurer and insured responsibilities.

Case analyses may be completed individually or in groups and may take place in class, outside of class, or in a combination of both, depending on the assignment. In-class case analyses are designed to encourage discussion, collaboration, and real-time application of course concepts. Students are expected to demonstrate critical thinking, clear reasoning, and professional communication consistent with industry standards. More detailed case materials, grading criteria, and instructions will be provided in class and on Canvas.

Attendance

Students are expected to attend all class sessions and arrive on time. Consistent attendance is strongly associated with success in class discussions, assignments, exams, and overall course performance. Attendance will be monitored throughout the semester, and adjustments to final points will be based on attendance records as outlined below.

  • Earn extra 40 points for having no absence throughout the semester. Thereafter, students 5 earn extra points less per excused absence without an unexcused absence. Thus, a student with five excused absences without any unexcused absence earns 15 extra points.

To have an absence classified as excused, students must submit written documentation within one week of the missed class. The documentation must be reviewed and approved by the instructor. Absences without proper documentation cannot be considered excused.

Honesty & Academic Integrity+

It is assumed that all students have read and understand ISU's academic integrity policy and the Code of Student Conduct (indstate.edu/sites/default/files/media/code_0.pdf). Infractions of this policy will result in actions as outlined in the policy.

It is students' responsibility to understand what constitutes plagiarism within an academic setting. Anyone involved in plagiarism or violation of other academic dishonesty in written assignments, quizzes, and final exam may result in the "F" grade for the course.

Any plagiarism in a written assignment will result in a zero grade. For a group assignment, plagiarism by a member will also result in zero for the group assignment when other members do not complete plagiarism. According to the Council of Writing Program Administrators (WPA), plagiarism is defined as

In an instructional setting, plagiarism occurs when a writer deliberately uses someone else's language, ideas, or other original (not common-knowledge) material without acknowledging its source.

This definition applies to texts published in print or online, to manuscripts, and to the work of other student writers.

Most current discussions of plagiarism fail to distinguish between:

  1. submitting someone else's text as one's own or attempting to blur the line between one's own ideas or words and those borrowed from another source, and
  2. carelessly or inadequately citing ideas and words borrowed from another source.

Such discussions conflate plagiarism with the misuse of sources.

Ethical writers make every effort to acknowledge sources fully and appropriately in accordance with the contexts and genres of their writing. A student who attempts (even if clumsily) to identify and credit his or her source, but who misuses a specific citation format or incorrectly uses quotation marks or other forms of identifying material taken from other sources, has not plagiarized. Instead, such a student should be considered to have failed to cite and document sources appropriately.

Source: Defining and Avoiding Plagiarism: The WPA Statement on Best Practices (wpacouncil.org/node/9)
